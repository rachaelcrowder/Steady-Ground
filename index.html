About the Programme For Participants

What is Steady Ground?

Training for those who hold others through difficult work.

Facilitators delivering education on tech-facilitated gender-based violence (TFGBV) regularly encounter participants who carry lived experience of the content being discussed—and they do so without structured preparation for that reality.

Steady Ground changes that. Designed by Rachael Crowder, PhD, RSW, this intensive programme draws on SAMHSA's six trauma-informed principles, Polyvagal Theory, Internal Family Systems, and intersectional feminist pedagogy to prepare facilitators not just with content knowledge, but with the relational and regulatory skills the work actually requires.

The training itself enacts the principles it teaches. Participants don't just learn about co-regulation and shared power—they experience them in the room.

"The room can only be as steady as the person at the front of it."

Steady Ground Programme

The CARE Protocol

C
Center
Self-regulate before you respond. One breath. Slow your pace. This is step zero—the one most facilitators skip.
A
Acknowledge
Validate without amplifying. Name what is happening—without making it bigger.
R
Restore
Return agency to the participant. Offer genuine options and mean them.
E
Enlist
Bring in your co-facilitator. Never leave a distressed participant without a point of contact.

The Framework

Grounded in evidence, built for practice.

Steady Ground integrates four evidence-based frameworks into a coherent facilitation practice. Each one informs both the content participants learn and the learning environment they experience.

SAMHSA

Six Trauma-Informed Principles

Safety, trustworthiness, peer support, collaboration, empowerment, and cultural humility—applied not only to survivor-facing work, but to the design of any learning environment where participants may carry lived experience of the content being discussed.

Polyvagal Theory

The Neuroscience of Safety

Porges' Polyvagal Theory describes the autonomic circuits governing human response. Facilitators learn to recognize activation cues in groups, use co-regulation as a facilitation tool, and create the neurological conditions under which learning is actually possible.

IFS · Parts-Informed

Protective Patterns in Groups

Internal Family Systems offers a non-pathologizing lens for understanding how participants respond to activating content—dominance, over-helping, checking out, humour—as adaptive protectors. A parts-informed facilitator meets these with curiosity rather than judgment.

Feminist Pedagogy

Intersectional Feminist Lens

GBV is produced by systems of power, not individuals. The programme maintains a structural, gender-transformative analysis throughout—and reflects the principle that the form of a learning experience teaches as much as its content.

The Programme

Two days. One cohort. Lasting practice.

Delivered as two consecutive three-hour in-person sessions, Steady Ground combines didactic teaching, modelling, supervised practice, and case-based learning in a fully co-facilitated format. Co-facilitation is not a contingency—it is pedagogy. Participants experience the co-regulation and shared power they are being trained to enact.

Day 1 · 3 Hours · Foundations

The Principles in Practice

Trauma-informed principles; Polyvagal basics; the TFGBV landscape; parts-informed facilitation; the co-regulation lab; micro-skills and invitational language; the CARE Protocol; case studies.

Day 2 · 3 Hours · Application

Rupture, Repair & Practice

Rupture and repair in context; role-play rounds with competency feedback; ethics and scope; evidence handling; trauma-informed presentation design; facilitator sustainability and care planning.

4–6 Weeks Post · 90 Minutes

Community of Practice

Structured peer consultation, facilitation debrief, design review, and renewal of care commitments. The evidence-based bridge between initial training and embedded practice change.

About the Facilitator

Rachael Crowder
PhD · RSW

Grounded in twenty years of trauma-informed practice.

Rachael Crowder is a clinician, clinical supervisor, and organizational consultant based in Prince Edward Island. Her practice sits at the intersection of trauma-informed care, feminist pedagogy, and organizational health, drawing on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, Internal Family Systems, Jungian analysis, and somatic approaches.

She held faculty appointments at the University of Calgary's Faculty of Social Work for ten years, designing curriculum and teaching across direct practice, trauma, and organizational theory. She has designed and delivered training for healthcare, legal, and social service professionals across Canada.

Steady Ground is the convergence of her clinical expertise, her commitment to feminist organizational practice, and her direct experience supporting GBV educators in this specific and demanding work.
