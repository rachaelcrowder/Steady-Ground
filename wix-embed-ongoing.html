Ongoing Learning

The training doesn't end when the room empties.

The Community of Practice session is the evidence-based bridge between what participants learn in the room and what they actually do in their facilitation practice. It is also where the real learning begins.

Community of Practice

Because practice change happens in between the sessions.

The research on train-the-trainer models is consistent on this point: the effectiveness of a training is not determined in the room. It is determined in the weeks that follow — when participants return to their organizations and try to apply what they learned without the training team in the room, without the cohort around them, and often without any structured support for the challenges they encounter.

The Steady Ground Community of Practice session addresses this directly. Held four to six weeks after Day 2, it is a 90-minute, co-facilitated session that gives participants a structured space to debrief their real facilitation experiences, consult with peers and facilitators, and renew their commitments to practice.

The CoP session is not a review of training content. It is a peer consultation that uses training frameworks to work through real situations participants have encountered in the field — and that begins from the assumption that each participant has already done something hard, and deserves support in making sense of it.

Format

90 minutes · co-facilitated · in-person or virtual

The CoP session can be held in person or virtually, depending on the cohort's needs. It is structured as peer consultation: participants bring situations they have encountered since the training and work through them using the frameworks and tools from both training days.

Structure

What happens in the room

The session opens with a brief check-in, moves through structured peer consultation on facilitation situations participants bring, includes a facilitation debrief for any completed presentations, reviews and renews personal care commitments, and closes with a design review for upcoming facilitation work. What the Evidence Says

Why follow-up makes the difference.

A 2023 study published in the Journal of Clinical Psychiatry found that trauma-informed training resulted in significant, sustained improvements in organizational knowledge, attitudes, and policies — but that these improvements were substantially stronger when training was accompanied by structured follow-up and peer consultation (Mangus et al., 2023).

Parallel research on train-the-trainer models in gender-based violence education consistently finds that the degree of practice change is predicted less by the quality of the initial training and more by the quality of the support structure that follows it (Şimşek & Uğur, 2024). A well-designed training without follow-up produces knowledge. A well-designed training with structured follow-up produces practice.

This is why the Community of Practice session is not an optional add-on to Steady Ground. It is part of the programme's design — the component that makes the investment in Days 1 and 2 pay off in the rooms that matter.

Participants who complete Steady Ground join a growing network of TFGBV educators committed to trauma-informed practice. The Steady Ground resource hub is available to all cohort participants throughout and beyond the cohort year.
